## Pancreatic cancer halted by virus injection in three patients

- 原文链接: [Pancreatic cancer halted by virus injection in three patients](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2528235-pancreatic-cancer-halted-by-virus-injection-in-three-patients/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50563066)

In a small US study, pancreatic tumors stopped growing in three people after doctors injected a virus into their tumors. The study was meant to check safety, so the result is encouraging, but it’s too early to tell if the treatment works.

Pancreatic cancer is often found after it has already spread. By then, doctors may not be able to remove it. These tumors also have a tough outer layer that makes it hard for some drugs to get in. The team used a virus changed to attack cancer cells while leaving healthy cells alone. When the three patients got the shot, their cancer had not spread beyond the pancreas. The first patient got the shot about a year before the team shared the results. All three were still alive when the team reported back. Their tumors hadn’t grown, but they hadn’t shrunk either. The team gave each person just a tenth of the dose it hopes to test. Next, it plans to try higher doses in 15 more people. It also wants to test the virus with drugs that help the body fight cancer. The US has already approved a different virus treatment for a type of skin cancer.

No Hacker News comments were provided, but the article offers two views on the results. Study leader Masato Yamamoto was surprised to see tumors stop growing at such a low dose. He thinks they might still shrink with more time. He also hopes that when cancer cells die, the body’s defenses will learn to spot the cancer. That could matter for people whose cancer has spread, but the study hasn’t shown that yet. Pancreatic surgeon Kai Brown takes a more careful view. He points out that many cancer treatments have looked good in small studies only to fall short in bigger ones. This study didn’t have a group of patients who went without the virus for comparison. So we don’t know whether the tumors would have stayed the same without the shot. That’s the question a larger study needs to answer.

## Claude solves two open problems in the physics of black holes

- 原文链接: [Claude solves two open problems in the physics of black holes](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50563148)

Anthropic says its AI, Claude, solved two open problems about black holes. Both are part of a bigger effort to explain how gravity works at very small scales.

The first problem is about a model universe that holds heat like a bowl. At low temperatures it is full of gas, but when it gets hot enough, a black hole takes over. In 1983, Stephen Hawking and Don Page showed that this change was sudden, like water starting to boil. But their math only worked when space and time had a clear shape. To study what happens when that shape gets blurry, Claude used another way to describe the same universe, with rules for tiny particles and no gravity. Physicists had set up the math in 2003, but it had thousands of parts and no one had finished it. Several copies of Claude worked out the pieces and checked each other’s work, and the answer was still a sudden switch.

The second problem takes place in a model universe with two dimensions of space instead of three. In that model, you can rule out a theory if it says the lightest black hole is heavier than a certain limit. Earlier computer work gave a good guess for that limit, but it could not prove the guess was right. Claude saw a pattern in the numbers and used a tool from another area of math to find the exact limit. It also proved that the simplest version of the test, which leaves out black hole spin, could not push the limit any lower. A program called Lean checked the second result, but neither result tells us how gravity works in our own universe.

Is the bigger story that Claude finished a long piece of math, or that it found a new way to solve a problem? On the first problem, a physicist working with Claude got the same answer by a different route. That’s a stronger check than just asking the same tool to do the same math twice. On the second problem, part of the proof followed an idea from earlier work by an OpenAI model. Does that make the result less surprising, or is moving an idea from one problem to another exactly what makes it interesting? There’s also the question of how much the people did. A researcher set up the AI workers, and a physicist checked the main results in the second paper. The AI workers had about six weeks and a list of 22 problems, so it would be useful to hear what happened on the other 20. Would this way of working help other research teams, or were these two problems an especially good fit?

## Garage Door Spam

- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50519231)

A garage door alert should tell you when the door opens, not try to sell you something. In “Garage Door Spam,” the writer explains why they stopped trusting Chamberlain’s myQ app and set up their own door controls.

The app already had ads, but the writer kept it because the alerts were useful. If a door opened while they were away, they knew to check what was going on. Then, in October 2026, an ad showed up as an alert.

Chamberlain had already cut off access for other apps in late 2023. The writer bought two small Wi-Fi boards called ratgdo32 for about $62 each. The boards connect to the existing door openers and work over the home network. They also work with Apple HomeKit, so the writer can ask Siri to open either door.

The project brought back an old idea from the writer’s dad. He had set up a light in the hall to show when the garage door was open. Now a small program checks the doors every ten minutes at night and sends a Slack message if a door was left open. The writer sent a small change to the project, and the person who runs it was happy to add it. With Tailscale, the writer can also check from away from home that both boards are still working. The doors no longer need Chamberlain’s online service, and the new alerts even say who opened the door.

## UK government forced to admit it issued Apple a secret encryption order

- 原文链接: [UK government forced to admit it issued Apple a secret encryption order](https://macanorak.com/uk-government-forced-to-admit-it-issued-apple-a-secret-encryption-order/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50562113)

A UK court has ruled that the government must admit it sent Apple a secret order about encrypted iCloud data. The ruling makes the fight more public, but it does not settle whether the government can make Apple change how it protects that data.

Under a 2016 law, the UK can order tech companies to change their services to help with investigations. It can also stop those companies from saying they received an order. In 2025, The Washington Post reported that the first order asked Apple to give the UK access to protected iCloud data from users around the world. The UK later dropped that order and reportedly sent a new one covering only UK users. Apple went back to court to fight the new order. Apple says it will not build a secret way into its services. Instead, it stopped letting more UK users turn on a setting called Advanced Data Protection. With that setting on, even Apple cannot read more kinds of iCloud data, such as backups. UK users who had already turned it on could keep it. So two people with the same phone and the same iCloud plan could get different levels of protection. The court said the government had not shown that admitting the order existed would put the country at risk. It did not say whether the order was legal or explain exactly what Apple was asked to do. A fuller hearing is expected in 2027.

No Hacker News comments were provided, so these are points for discussion, not a report on the thread. The government says it needs these powers to help with legal investigations and keep people safe. It also says that naming the companies that got orders could help people who want to harm the country. Rights groups take a different view. They say it is hard to check how the government uses its power if it will not even admit it sent an order. For customers, it is worth noting that iCloud still uses encryption without the extra setting. The difference is that Apple can still read some of that data and may have to hand it over if the law requires it. For developers, the bigger question is what happens when the same law is used against another cloud service. How would customers know if a secret order changed who could read their data? And how much could the government tell the public without giving away details of an investigation?

- 原文链接: [Someone reported java4shell bug in September 2026](https://github.com/openjdk/jdk/pull/32781)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50558906)

On September 9, 2026, an OpenJDK developer reported a bug in Java’s fast Base64 decoder. The report came with a proposed fix for a part of Java that uses a CPU feature called AVX-512.

Base64 is a way to turn data into text. The decoder turns that text back into data. On some computers, Java can do this faster by working on many bytes at once. The bug was in the code that handles the last few bytes. The report says that when the ending is not valid, Java can get the size of the result wrong. It can then write past the space set aside for that result. That can damage other data in memory. The GitHub page links to several reports of Java crashes. Those links alone do not prove that this bug caused the crashes. The proposed fix lets the normal Java code handle that short ending instead. The headline calls the bug “java4shell,” but the report does not show that someone can use it to run their own code. The page also does not say which Java releases got a fix.

## Launch HN: Onlook (YC W25) – An open-source developer tool for designers

- 原文链接: [Launch HN: Onlook (YC W25) – An open-source developer tool for designers](https://github.com/onlook-dev/onlook/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50562320)

Onlook is an open-source desktop tool that lets designers work on real websites and apps. The team introduced it on Hacker News as an alternative to tools like Figma and Webflow.

Instead of making a drawing that a developer has to rebuild later, you work with the app itself. Onlook opens a Git project and runs it on your computer. You can see phone, tablet, and desktop views side by side. You can click a part of the page and tell the AI what you want to change. For example, you might click a button and ask the AI to make it easier to see. Onlook does not come with its own AI, so you connect Claude Code or ChatGPT Codex. The AI edits the project files, and you can see the result as the app reloads. Onlook works in a separate Git copy, so your main project folder stays untouched. You can also make quick sketches outside the project and share them as a web page, a picture, or a PDF. When a change is ready, you can open a pull request for your team to review. The desktop app is available for Mac, but there is no download for Windows or Linux yet. If you build the app from its source code, you do not need an Onlook account. The Mac download collects data about how you use the app, but you can turn that off in Settings.
