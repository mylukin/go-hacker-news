## Gemini 4 Neon

- 原文链接: [Gemini 4 Neon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50552424)

Google has announced Gemini 4 Neon, an AI model for hard jobs in coding, business, and science. It sits between Hydrogen, which is built for quick everyday tasks, and Carbon, which is built for the hardest jobs.

Google says Neon can finish many complex jobs faster and at a lower cost than bigger models. It can work with spreadsheets, PDFs, charts, audio, and video in the same task. It can handle up to one million tokens, or small pieces of data, at once. The API costs three dollars per million tokens you send and fifteen dollars per million tokens it sends back. If Google can reuse input you already sent, that input costs just fifteen cents per million tokens.

At its highest thinking setting, Neon came third on one broad AI test. It came first on one set of coding tests and on tests for legal and tax research. Google says it can fix bugs across several files without making large changes it doesn’t need to make. In Google’s Antigravity coding tool, Neon can also give parts of a job to smaller Hydrogen models. It has four thinking settings, so you can spend less on simple jobs and give it more time for hard ones. With its safety checks on, Neon ignored every hidden instruction in one test, but that doesn’t mean it will catch them all in real use. Neon is rolling out through Google’s paid API, its business tools, and the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra users.

The Hacker News comments weren’t included, so we can’t report what people there thought. Still, the launch raises a few questions worth talking about. If you run the same job hundreds of times a day, a lower cost per job could matter more than a higher test score. For a hard job that only comes up once, you might still pay more for Carbon. The coding results raise another question: how much of the gain comes from Neon, and how much comes from Google’s coding tool? A good test would be to give it an old bug in your own project and see how much work is left for you. The large input limit sounds useful, but reading a long bill is not the same as finding the one line that explains a price jump. The discount for reused input could be a real plus if you send the same files every day. Some teams might be happy to let Neon work on its own while it reads files and writes a report. Those same teams might still want it to ask before it changes code or sends an email.

## Unauthenticated remote code execution in Nginx

- 原文链接: [Unauthenticated remote code execution in Nginx](https://www.wiz.io/blog/nginx-http2-rce-vulnerabilities)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50547374)

Wiz reports two serious bugs in NGINX that can let someone run code on a server without logging in. NGINX often acts as the front door for several websites and apps, so one affected server can put many services at risk. Both bugs need HTTP/2 to be turned on. In the first bug, NGINX frees memory that it still needs to handle a request. In the second, a mistake in how NGINX counts data can cause it to copy data outside the right area in memory. Wiz tested the second bug on a server that did nothing more than serve ordinary files. That means your own app code can be safe and you can still be at risk. One of the bugs had been in NGINX since 2015. Wiz says it used AI tools to help find and check both bugs. Several teams reported the bugs to F5, the company behind NGINX. F5 released fixes for both bugs, along with other security fixes. Wiz says to update servers that anyone on the internet can reach first. If you can’t update right away, you can turn off HTTP/2 for now. Test that your site still works after the change. Once you’ve installed the fix, you can turn HTTP/2 back on.

No Hacker News comments were included, so here are a few questions the story raises. If AI helps find more old bugs, can teams install fixes fast enough to keep up? Finding a bug is one job, but testing an update across many services is another. For a small team, turning off HTTP/2 may be the fastest way to stay safe until an update is ready. For a team that depends on HTTP/2, getting the fix out quickly may be the better choice. There’s also a question about how much to share when a bug goes public. More detail can help people understand what went wrong, but Wiz chose not to share the code it used to prove the attacks worked. And that simple file server is worth thinking about. If one shared server can put several well-built apps at risk, who’s in charge of keeping it up to date? That’s a question teams can answer before the next security alert.

## Seven new models at scalable price-performance

- 原文链接: [Seven new models at scalable price-performance](https://microsoft.ai/news/microsoft-ignite-2026/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50548021)

Microsoft AI announced seven new models at its Ignite event. The big promise is that teams can run AI tools day and night without a huge bill.

Microsoft says the cost of all the text these tools read and write can add up fast. In its coding tests, MAI Code 2 got 98 percent of GPT-6.1 Sol’s score at about 40 percent of the price. It also got 96 percent of Opus 5.5’s score at just 5 percent of the price. Code 2 Flash got close to GPT-6 Luna and Haiku 5.5 in the tests and cost less than both. Microsoft says Cyber 2 Flash beat GPT-6 Astra in one security test at less than 2 percent of the cost per task. Image 2.7 took first place in an image editing test and cost one fifth as much as the OpenAI model Microsoft points to. Realtime 1 starts at one cent per minute of audio. MAI Transcribe 2 turns speech into text in many languages and runs inside Dragon Copilot Transcribe. Microsoft says the tool helps with 10 million patient visits a month. It says the tool now makes half as many mistakes when it turns speech into text across languages. Microsoft also says its Decision model is 50 times as fast as GPT-6.1 Sol and costs about one seventy-ninth as much. The company is already using its new Maia 200 chip to run its biggest models. Microsoft says the chip does 70 percent more AI work for the same amount of power.

The Hacker News comments weren’t included, so these are open questions, not views from the thread. For a small team, a cheaper coding model could make it easier to run routine checks all day. But if people spend more time fixing its work, some of those savings could go away. A test score is a good place to start, but will the model do as well on your own code? It’s also worth asking what one finished job costs, not just what one try costs. For a voice app, one cent per minute is easy to plan around. But if the app takes too long to answer, a low price won’t make it feel better to use. In a doctor’s office, fewer mistakes sounds good, but which mistakes does the tool still make? The chip numbers raise a different question: will Microsoft pass any of those power savings on to customers? A team might end up using the cheaper model for simple jobs and paying more only when the work gets hard.

## DaisyRose-27B: 1,200-task RL took Qwen3.8 from 5.6% to 12% on Terminal-Bench 4.1

- 原文链接: [DaisyRose-27B: 1,200-task RL took Qwen3.8 from 5.6% to 12% on Terminal-Bench 4.1](https://www.radixark.com/blog/daisyrose-27b)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50551046)

RadixArk built DaisyRose-27B by giving Qwen3.8-27B extra training on 1,200 carefully picked tasks. On Terminal-Bench 4.1, a test of long jobs done from the command line, its first-try success rate went from 5.6% to 12%.

The team first ran Qwen on each task to see where it went wrong. They changed the tasks to bring out those mistakes and made sure each job was neither too easy nor too hard. Each job had several steps, so a mistake early on could cause trouble later. The team also fixed or dropped tasks that the model could pass without doing the real work. The training run took 18 days on 144 H200 GPUs. Most of the GPUs ran the model through jobs while the rest used those attempts to teach it, so neither group had to wait for the other. Some work sessions ran to a million tokens. RadixArk says the full setup worked about twice as fast as its open-source setup on the same hardware. Each attempt ran in a fresh space where the model could run commands and edit files. The team used about 944,000 of these spaces and kept costs down by giving each one only as much memory as it needed. The model also scored better on the coding, reasoning, and instruction tests shown in the post. For Terminal-Bench 4.1, the team ran each task eight times to estimate how often the model would pass on its first try. The model is free to download from Hugging Face in three formats.

No Hacker News comments were included, so here are some questions the results raise. Is more than doubling the score the big story, or is it that the model still fails most jobs? For a developer, that may depend on whether checking the model’s work saves time compared with doing the job by hand. A small team might like the idea of teaching a model with just 1,200 tasks. But picking and checking those tasks takes work, and 144 high-end GPUs are still out of reach for many teams. It would be useful to know how much of the gain came from the choice of tasks and how much came from letting the model work for longer. Another question is whether the gains hold up on jobs that look very different from the training tasks. The speed numbers also raise a question about what other teams can repeat, since the fastest setup used RadixArk’s own training system. For teams already doing this kind of training, the way RadixArk shared memory across jobs could be as useful as the new model. And now that the model is out, developers can try it on their own work and see where the higher score makes a difference.

## AI agents ask better questions than KPMG accountants in audit trial

- 原文链接: [AI agents ask better questions than KPMG accountants in audit trial](https://europeanbusinessmagazine.com/ai-agents-better-questions-kpmg/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50547777)

KPMG tested whether AI agents could help check the accounts of a large UK company. The firm says the agents sometimes looked deeper and asked better questions than its own accountants.

More than 100 AI agents worked alongside the human team. They gathered records, checked the numbers, and wrote notes about what they found. They also shared tasks and suggested what to check next. In some cases, they raised questions the human team had not asked. But one agent failed to match the audit rules to the company’s records. A human auditor had to step in. A senior auditor signed off on the test, but KPMG did not use the results to officially approve the company’s accounts.

KPMG plans to try the tools on up to 20 larger audits. One problem is that some companies do not keep their records in a form the agents can easily use. If the tools work well, they could take on some of the repeated checks that junior accountants do today. That could give senior staff more time for hard decisions. KPMG has not said whether saving time would mean lower bills for clients. Whatever the AI finds, a human auditor is still responsible for the final decision.

## DNS resolvers are the wrong place to fight piracy

- 原文链接: [DNS resolvers are the wrong place to fight piracy](https://techstackups.com/articles/quad9-leaves-france-dns-blocking/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50545958)

Quad9, a free service that helps people reach websites safely, plans to stop serving users in France on November 24, 2026. The article argues that a French court picked the wrong tool to fight illegal sports streams.

A DNS resolver finds the internet address that goes with a website name. Quad9 does this without keeping user records, and it blocks sites known to steal passwords or spread harmful software. French law lets sports companies ask a court to block sites that show live games without permission. In this case, a list of sites showing women’s tennis grew from six names to 58. The court set a fine of one thousand euros a day after Quad9 refused to block them. Quad9 says it cannot afford more fines with about twenty other cases still open. The court pointed out that Quad9 already blocks harmful sites around the world. But Quad9 says it cannot reliably tell which users are in France, so blocking the names could affect people in other countries. A DNS block does not take a stream offline; it just makes it harder to find. It can also block a whole website when only one part is breaking the law. France says sports piracy fell by four percent in 2025, but its numbers do not show how much DNS blocking helped. Meanwhile, hospitals and other groups that miss the news could find that websites no longer load.

On Hacker News, one reader said the companies that run pirate sites can ignore court orders, so courts have to look for someone else who can help. Another asked Quad9 where courts should step in instead. The author’s answer was to start with the companies that host the streams, since a study found that most European piracy is hosted in Europe. Some readers said a block can still help, because most people will not bother to switch DNS services. The author pushed back, noting that one in five French internet users already uses a different DNS service. Other readers said Cloudflare was in a poor position to complain because another of its services helps deliver pirate sites to viewers. The author said that raises a fair question about that service, not about DNS. One reader asked if a phone book should keep listing a drug dealer’s new address. The author said DNS is more like the phone service that connects your call than a book that chooses what to list. Quad9’s John Todd asked readers to write to lawmakers, warning that the rest of Europe might follow France’s lead.

## Advancing chip design with OpenAI models

- 原文链接: [Advancing chip design with OpenAI models](https://openai.com/index/advancing-chip-design-with-our-models/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50549493)

OpenAI says its AI models helped make parts of real chips smaller or faster. The report covers OpenAI’s Jalapeño chip, its work with AMD, and tests on two open-source processors.

On Jalapeño, the models found ways for different steps to share the same hardware. One circuit that multiplies numbers took up about 15% less space and went from two clock cycles to one. Across two larger parts of the chip, OpenAI estimates that the small circuits took up about 13% and 10% less space, and it says the changes worked on real chips. On an AMD graphics chip, OpenAI reports gains of up to 14% over designs that engineers had already worked hard to improve. The team looked at power use, speed, and size, so that number does not mean the whole chip ran 14% faster. The model used tools from Synopsys to check that the new circuits still did the same jobs. AMD sent a design with some of those changes to be made and saw smaller gains on parts of a future processor.

In its own tests, OpenAI asked models to make two RISC-V processors run more instructions per second. A design got zero points if it failed a check or took up more than 16 times as much space as the original. With similar limits on how much work each model could do, GPT-6.2 Astra had the best average score on both processors. All five models scored better when they built from written rules instead of changing the old designs. But the new designs could leave out features the old ones had. OpenAI and Synopsys now plan to train a model to use the tools that chip designers use every day.

No Hacker News comments were included, so these are possible points for discussion, not reports of what readers said. One reason to be excited is that AMD put some of the changes into a design it sent to be made. That matters more than a high test score alone. A more careful view is that a gain in one part may make little difference to the whole chip. It would help to see separate numbers for power, speed, and size. The processor tests raise a different question: is a new design really better if it leaves out features? A tighter size limit could also change which designs win. Checking speed after laying out the circuits would make the test closer to real chip work. For engineers, another useful question is how much time it takes to check and fix the model’s work. The next thing to watch is whether other chip teams can get similar gains without spending more time on those checks.

## Search Browser – Minimalist WebKit browser with Chrome extension support

- 原文链接: [Search Browser – Minimalist WebKit browser with Chrome extension support](https://searchbrowser.app/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50551398)

Search is a free, open-source Mac browser that tries to keep the page in focus. It comes from a small design studio in Paris and works on Macs with macOS 14 or later, including older Intel Macs.

It uses WebKit, the same engine as Safari, so it does not need to bring its own. The download is about 11 megabytes. In the maker’s tests, the first window opened in about a third of a second, faster than Chrome or Safari on the same Mac. Search also used less memory before opening its first page, though that test does not show how it runs after a full day of work. You can put tabs across the top or down the side. You can pin a site so it stays even if the app is forced to close. Spaces keep sets of tabs apart, so you can switch from work to home with a two-finger swipe. Reading mode leaves just the article and its pictures. A video can follow you in a small window when you leave its tab. Search blocks ads before they load and lets you hide parts of a page you do not want to see again. On macOS 15.4 or later, you can install extensions from the Chrome Web Store, even though the browser uses WebKit. You can bring over bookmarks, history, and passwords from other browsers. You can also share tabs in Spaces, pins, and bookmarks across your Macs, but not your history or passwords.

The Hacker News comments weren’t included, so these are open questions, not a summary of the thread. The big one is which Chrome extensions work well enough to use every day. If even one tool you need for work fails, the small download may not matter much. On the other hand, if Safari already does most of what you need, adding a favorite Chrome extension could be enough to make you try Search. The speed test raises a different question: does the browser still feel light with twenty tabs open? That may matter more than how fast the first window appears. The sidebar and Spaces might be the bigger draw if you spend all day moving between projects. If you mostly browse with a few tabs, those tools may not change much. And keeping passwords out of sync may appeal to people who want less data shared between their Macs. For someone who switches between Macs all day, that same choice may mean more work.

## Google Gemini's "Tasks" Personal Assistant Is Now Free

- 原文链接: [Google Gemini's "Tasks" Personal Assistant Is Now Free](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/)
- HN链接: [Hacker News讨论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50551601)

Google says it’s making Gemini’s Tasks feature free, so the AI can do things like make calls and book appointments for you. It’s rolling out in six countries, including the United States, where some new features will arrive first.

In Google’s car repair example, Gemini calls two shops at the same time and waits on hold. It tells each shop that it’s an AI, then books a time that fits the owner’s calendar. In another example, the owner gives Gemini access to a power company website and asks it to check past bills in Gmail. Gemini finds that the heat pump often runs when power costs the most. Gemini can also compare products, find a discount, and help you pay with Google Pay. You can set a spending limit, and Google says Gemini is meant to ask before buying unless you’ve already said yes. In the United States, you can also text it a photo of an empty cereal box and ask it to add cereal to your grocery cart. You still finish that order in the store’s app. You can also send Gemini school emails and have it put the dates on your calendar. Google says Gemini works in a separate space for your account, and you can cut off its access to apps at any time. Google says it also checks web pages for text that tries to trick Gemini into doing something you didn’t ask for. Some tasks can still fail, and some sites may leave you to press the last button yourself. To try it, switch from Chat to Task in the Gemini app.

No Hacker News comments came with the article, so these are possible views, not a report on the thread. If you hate waiting on hold, the car repair example may be enough to make you try it. But a small shop may not want to spend time answering calls from bots. Google says shops can choose not to take those calls. Some people may like that Gemini can use the Google apps they already have. Others may not want one tool to see their mail, calendar, and payment details. Asking before a purchase sounds useful, but even a free task can go wrong if Gemini books the wrong date. For developers, the handoff raises a practical question: can a site let an AI fill out a form while leaving the final choice to the person? And when Gemini picks a product, will it show why that choice is best for you? For many people, the real test may be whether checking Gemini’s work takes less time than doing the task themselves.
